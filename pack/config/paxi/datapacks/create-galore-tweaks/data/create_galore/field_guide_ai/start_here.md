# Start Here (start_here)

## start_here/welcome | Welcome to Create Galore
- Big long-term Minecraft pack built around Create; rotating machines are the core. Also: airships, submarines, trains, roller coasters, the Moon, survival layers, homesteading, lots of new world.
- New spawn: read Your First Days (see start_here/first_days) before first night (drinking, clean water, food, warmth).
- The book is a wiki, not a quest list; nothing says what to do next. Each entry: one idea, how it behaves, rules that trip people up, connections to other mods.
- Reading pages: purple words (see glossary/index) open a definition in Words to Know; blue words open the full entry. "Key points" start a long entry (must-know part); "In depth" marks optional detail, all after it can wait. "Watch out" = danger or lost progress; "Tip" = useful trick.
- Sections: Start Here, Create: The Basics, How Things Connect, Industry and Power, Ores and Metals, Survival Layers, Trains and Rides, Flight, The Road to the Stars, Space, The Ocean, Exploration, Food and Farming, Home and Storage.
- Maps: each section is a map; an arrow from entry A to B means A usually comes first, so follow lines back to reach something. Drag to move, scroll to zoom. Magnifying glass on the right searches the text of every page.
- New to Create? Rotation (see create_basics/rotation) is the first idea.
- Ask the Book: on a home server whose owner gave the book a voice, the Ask the Book ribbon (speech bubble on the right edge of the book) opens pages to ask in your own words; also /askbook. Answers from this guide, the server's recipes, what is on your screen, what you carry, chests you opened nearby, your advancements.
- The book shows recipes, draws diagrams or builds 3D models you can turn (right-click one to keep it as a page). Ask why a build doesn't work: it finds the build near you and reads it block by block. It only sees blocks players placed, so it never finds ores or loot, and never gives map locations.
- Ask it to improve or fix a build: it copies the build into a 3D model with the changes outlined (green: add or change, red: take out). Hovering a 3D model shows a Save as schematic button: the model goes into the player's own schematics folder as a Create schematic; an Empty Schematic (create:empty_schematic) in a Schematic Table (create:schematic_table) turns it into a Schematic (create:schematic), as with any schematic. Hold the Schematic and right-click to place it, move/turn/mirror it with Create's schematic tools (hold Left Alt to pick one); then with it in the off hand the build stays shown while placing blocks, each block vanishes once the right one is in its spot, a wrong block gets a red outline. Pinning: sneak-right-click a placed Schematic (any Create schematic) and press Pin for everyone; it stays in the world as faint blocks with nothing held, every player on the server sees it, blocks placed right fill their spots and a wrong block turns red; Unpin on that screen or /schematic unpin near it takes it down, /schematic list lists pins (up to 120000 blocks per pin). It can answer on up to three pages at once; an answer on a page you aren't reading (or with the book closed) brings a notice, and the page glows green under All pages until opened; until then the Field Guide item (hotbar, inventory) and the Ask the Book ribbon glow green too.
- The Field Guide [minecraft:writable_book]: how to get/open it (see home/guide_shelf). See Tools of the Trade (see start_here/tools).

## start_here/first_days | Your First Days
- Three things can kill a new player that can't in vanilla: thirst, cold or heat, dirty water. All easy once you know the trick.
- Three threats: thirst (see survival/thirst), cold/heat (see survival/temperature), dirty water (see glossary/purified_water).
- Drinking, extras beyond (see survival/thirst): rain: drink a little every 0.5 s (every half second), free. By hand: quick sip, but raw water usually makes you sick.
- Lake, sea and swamp water is Dirty (see glossary/purified_water). Watch out: drinking it always gives hunger and nausea, and 3 times in 10 poison too; you still get the water. Flowing water, and water above Y (see glossary/y_level) 100 or below Y 48, is one step cleaner.
- First bottles [minecraft:glass_bottle]: smelt sand into glass, craft 3 glass into 3 bottles. Right-click water to fill, hold right-click to drink. Water bottles stack to 64.
- Boiling: furnace Dirty -> Acceptable directly (safe to live on); campfire one step per cook, slow, no fuel (see survival/thirst). Tip: boil a stack while you work.
- Food that hydrates: Melon slices [minecraft:melon_slice] best early pick (see food/first_food). At 3 droplets or less you can't sprint; at zero you take damage (see survival/thirst).
- Hunger works like vanilla: kill animals, cook meat on campfire or furnace, or fish. AppleSkin shows what food restores (see survival/food_and_health). What to eat where: Food in Your First Days (see food/first_food). Crops only grow in their season: Seasonal Crops (see survival/seasons_crops).
- Cold and heat: body temperature on the HUD; snowy spawn has about 5 minutes grace (see glossary/grace) then cold hurts; warm up at campfire, lit furnace or lava; getting wet chills you (helps in a desert) (see survival/temperature).
- Dress: Leather armor and furs insulate; Sewing Table [cold_sweat:sewing_table] (wool and planks) sews Goat Fur [cold_sweat:goat_fur] onto armor; Hearth for a base (see survival/temperature_gear). You can't sleep while freezing or overheating.
- Day one checklist: glass bottles filled and boiled; campfire (light, warmth, clean water); meat or melons; warm armor if snowy; a bed and a roof. Rain is free water.
- Related: Thirst (see survival/thirst), Temperature (see survival/temperature), Insulation and Heaters (see survival/temperature_gear), Seasonal Crops (see survival/seasons_crops), Food and Health (see survival/food_and_health), Food in Your First Days (see food/first_food), What to Expect (see start_here/difficulty)

## start_here/where_to_begin | How Ideas Build Up
- Not a checklist: a map of how ideas depend on each other; every playthrough orders differently.
- Rotation first: almost every machine needs spinning shafts. Read Rotation (see create_basics/rotation), Stress (see create_basics/stress), Power Sources (see create_basics/power_sources) before building anything that processes items (see create_basics/processing).
- Andesite Alloy [create:andesite_alloy]: first Create material; early machines and casings (see glossary/encased) use it. Brass (see create_basics/brass_and_assembly) comes later and opens the second tier. See Materials (see create_basics/materials).
- Survival pressure starts early: temperature and thirst from day one, seasons change crops, pollution grows with machines. See Temperature (see survival/temperature), Thirst (see survival/thirst), Seasons (see survival/seasons), Pollution (see survival/pollution).
- Vehicles need destinations: Flight Destinations (see flight/destinations), Submarines (see ocean/submarines), Trains (see transport/trains), Reaching Space (see space/reaching_space) are the long-range goals.
- Metals link everything: zinc, brass, steel, lead, titanium turn up in several mods. Ore Guide (see materials/ore_guide) says where each comes from; Electricity (see industry/electricity) and Steel (see industry/tfmg_steel) cover heavy industry tiers.
- Good starting links: Rotation (see create_basics/rotation), Core Materials (see create_basics/materials), Brass and Assembly (see create_basics/brass_and_assembly), Temperature (see survival/temperature), Reaching Space (see space/reaching_space)

## start_here/difficulty | What to Expect
- Combat is exciting, not punishing; bosses and dungeons are for adventure, not grind. Not the pack's focus if you want brutal fights.
- Pressure comes from four survival layers; each designed so machines can solve it; none meant to be a constant fight against the weather (see survival/temperature).
  - Temperature (see survival/temperature); clothing and heated rooms help.
  - Thirst (see survival/thirst): water is a need.
  - Seasons (see survival/seasons): crops grow in their season.
  - Pollution (see survival/pollution): burners and engines fill the sky over their chunks (see glossary/chunk) with smog; exhaust systems with filters clean it (see survival/pollution_control).
- Dangers also in new worlds: Bosses (see exploration/bosses), Structures (see exploration/structures) before exploring far; Dimensions (see exploration/dimensions).
- Watch out: snowy spawn, freezing damage after grace (see glossary/grace): insulated clothing + heat source (see survival/temperature) (see survival/temperature_gear).
- Fights [minecraft:iron_sword]: bosses and dungeon mobs meant to be memorable, not wear you down. Biggest, Cataclysm's Netherite Monstrosity, has 420 health; most bosses 100 to 315 (see exploration/bosses).

## start_here/performance | Performance and Shaders
- Heavy pack. Give the game 8 GB RAM on a 16 GB PC, or 12 GB on a big one (launcher instance settings). More than 16 GB doesn't help. README has matching Java flags.
- Distant Horizons [minecraft:spyglass]: draws far terrain as simplified chunks (see glossary/chunk); see much further than normal render distance. Useful for airships, submarine, the Moon; costs memory and some frame rate. Holes near you while flying fast fill in once chunks arrive. Tip: turn its settings down first if the game stutters.
- Laptop (16 GB RAM): game 8 GB, no more; play plugged in; Distant Horizons render distance 128 or lower (pack ships 256); render distance 10, simulation distance 8; skip shaders or use Solas (lightest); don't host the server on it while you play.
- Shaders (optional, via Iris): Options > Video Settings > Shader Packs. Complementary Reimagined (with Euphoria Patches), Bliss, Photon, Solas. For Bliss or Photon pick the copy ending in "+ Clrwl" so contraptions are shaded right. Shaders can cut frame rate a lot on weaker machines.
- DLAA (optional): only with the shader pack "Photon v1.3a + DLAA", a copy of Photon + Clrwl the pack makes at startup; pick it under Shader Packs. Replaces Photon's own anti-aliasing (TAA) with NVIDIA DLSS or AMD FSR 3.1: sharper, far less shimmer on leaves, fences, water. At Native (full resolution, DLAA) it costs about 1.2 ms per frame on an RTX 4080 at 1440p; below native it upscales for more frames.
- DLAA render resolution (settings screen): Native (100%), Quality (67%), Balanced (58%), Performance (50%), Ultra Performance (33%), Custom (slider 25-100% of the window's width and height). Below native Photon renders the scene smaller and DLSS or FSR 3.1 upscale it to the window; with anti-aliasing Off, Photon's own upscaler (TAAU) does it, soft and smeary at these sizes. Tested on an RTX 4080 Super at 2560x1440: DLSS Quality looks like native and runs about as fast as Photon's TAA at full resolution (15-20% faster than DLAA); DLSS Performance looks like a slightly softer 1440p (25-31% faster than DLAA), reflections and shadows under moving things settle a moment later; FSR 3.1 best at Quality (below that it stays smooth but far leaves crawl and particles trail); Ultra Performance not recommended. Recommendation: RTX card -> DLSS Quality (free frames) or Performance; other cards -> FSR Quality.
- DLAA settings: Upscaling tab of the Upscaling & Display screen (Upscaling & Display... button at the top of the shader pack menu, or Mods > Create Galore Bridge > Config). Anti-aliasing Auto (NVIDIA DLAA on an RTX card with nvngx_dlss.dll, else FSR 3.1), NVIDIA DLAA, AMD FSR 3.1 native, Off (Photon's TAA). DLSS preset Default (K), K, J, L, M, or any typed letter (one DLSS has no preset for runs as default). Changes reload the shaders. K is the default at every render resolution; L (made for Ultra Performance) and M (made for Performance) are worth trying from Balanced down, L sharpest at Performance; L and M also keep Photon's underwater light shafts and particles (and campfire embers) clean, where K and FSR smear them slightly (not a bug). L and M need FP8, native on RTX 40/50 only; RTX 20/30 emulate it and lose much of the gain, so stay on K there if frames drop (the settings screen warns).
- DLSS needs the player's own nvngx_dlss.dll (not part of the pack), DLSS 310.9.16 tested: the settings screen has Find on this PC (only when pressed, looks only in %ProgramData%\NVIDIA\NGX\models\dlss, where the NVIDIA app keeps the DLSS versions it downloaded for DLSS Override or game updates, and copies the newest into the DLSS folder so NVIDIA removing theirs changes nothing; none there = it says so; a DLL in use is replaced at the next start), Get from NVIDIA (NVIDIA's DLSS SDK on GitHub), Open DLSS folder (create-galore-dlss in the game folder) and shows the found version; close the game before replacing it, reload shaders (F8) after adding it. FSR 3.1 needs nothing (any card with OpenGL 4.5).
- DLAA can't run (no RTX card, missing DLL, a Photon option it doesn't cover such as Photon's TAA turned off): a notice says why, Photon keeps its TAA, settings screen status line says it too. Engine that closed the game while starting stays off until Try again on the settings screen. Log lines start with "DLAA:" (graphics card, engine, preset, why off).
- First world creation is slow (many mods add world generation); settles after first load.
- Name tags are drawn after the shader, readable with every pack.
- Fullscreen F11; screenshot F2.
- Ambiance runs only on your PC: cave echo, footsteps, falling leaves, held torches that light up, smoother animations. Changes nothing in play: a torch in hand looks bright but doesn't stop mobs spawning; place one for that. Fresh Animations can be turned off under Resource Packs.
- Chunksmith pregenerates terrain on the /cs commands, ahead of an airship or train; yields to players so you can keep playing. Its automatic run on world entry (4096-block radius every join) is switched off here.
- NVIDIA card + 10-bit monitor: set Vulkan/OpenGL present method to Prefer layered on DXGI Swapchain in NVIDIA Control Panel, or the driver still switches a 10-bit monitor to 8-bit. With shaders this can cap the GPU at about half speed; set back to Auto if you don't need 10-bit.
- Black strobing after that change on some GPUs/drivers: appears when VSync (or adaptive VSync) is on and Max Framerate is Unlimited. Fix: VSync off (RivaTuner can then cap FPS smoothly), or cap Max Framerate at or below monitor refresh rate.
- Perfect Presentation (Windows, experimental): shows game through DirectX; fullscreen is a fast borderless window, Alt-Tab instant, 10-bit stays 10-bit. Turn on in the display settings, Display tab (Upscaling & Display... button in the shader pack menu, or Mods > Create Galore Bridge > Config; saved as perfectPresentation=true in config (see glossary/config) file config/create_galore_bridge-display.properties), restart once (set by hand in the file it takes two restarts; the screen also turns off NeoForge's early loading window). On NVIDIA set Power management mode to Prefer maximum performance for javaw.exe.
- DirectX (Display tab, saved as presenter=dx11|dx12): what Perfect Presentation shows the game through. 11 is the default; 12 is experimental and not recommended as the default yet. In tests (RTX 4080 Super, 1440p) 12 ran at the same frame rate as 11 (within 0-3%) with marginally lower input delay (0.5-1.0 ms), G-SYNC working on both. Picking 12 turns Perfect Presentation on, restart once; turning Perfect Presentation off sets DirectX back to 11. Needs an OpenGL driver that shares textures with DirectX 12 (NVIDIA's does, Intel's doesn't, AMD's untested); where 12 can't start the game runs on 11 and the screen says so beside the setting (log lines start with "Perfect Presentation:"). Reflex, GPU pacing and the frame counter work the same on both.
- NVIDIA Reflex (Windows, NVIDIA GTX 900 or newer, experimental): display settings screen, Display tab, NVIDIA Reflex Off / On / On + Boost (saved as reflex=off|on|boost). The driver starts each frame as late as it can while keeping the card busy and the game reads the mouse just before drawing, so the view follows the mouse sooner; On + Boost also keeps the card's clocks up (helps where the processor holds the frame rate back, more power). Runs on Perfect Presentation's DirectX device: picking it turns Perfect Presentation on, restart once; turning Perfect Presentation off turns Reflex off. With a Max Framerate set, Reflex keeps that cap (measured on an RTX 4080 at 1440p: 8.7 ms input to frame done at a 60 fps cap). Uncapped it only reads the mouse later (10.5-11.9 ms), because the driver can't see the OpenGL rendering; GPU pacing does more there. Perfect Presentation alone also lowers latency (14.3 vs 18.8 ms). Beside the setting the screen says whether it runs (driver confirms low latency mode), waits for a restart, or why it can't (no NVIDIA card, not Windows). If the game closed while Reflex started, it stays off until Try again on that screen. Log lines start with "Reflex:".
- Upscaling & Display screen (Upscaling & Display... button at the top of the shader pack menu, or Mods > Create Galore Bridge > Config) has two tabs: Upscaling (anti-aliasing engine, DLSS preset, render resolution, nvngx_dlss.dll) and Display (Perfect Presentation, 10-bit window, DirectX 11 or 12, NVIDIA Reflex, GPU pacing, frame counter).
- Max Framerate (any card): the cap waits at the start of each frame, before the game reads the mouse, so a cap the card can hold gives the shortest input delay: 8.8 ms input to frame done at a 60 fps cap on an RTX 4080 at 1440p with Perfect Presentation, the same as Reflex's cap. Reflex's own cap takes over while Reflex runs.
- GPU pacing (any card, Display tab, saved as gpuPacing=off|on|lowest): for playing uncapped. The game waits just before drawing each frame so the frame reaches the card about when the card is ready, instead of queueing behind earlier frames while its mouse input ages, and reads the mouse again just before drawing. On keeps a short queue (65% of a frame, at least 4 ms, with Perfect Presentation; 80%, at least 5 ms, without) and costs no frames; Lowest keeps the queue as short as the frames' timing allows, about 6% fewer frames. Measured on an RTX 4080 at 1440p with DLAA, input to frame done (median/90th percentile): with Perfect Presentation 11.9/14.5 ms off, 9.8/10.5 On, 8.4/8.9 Lowest (Reflex On uncapped 10.5/11.9); without it 14.4/18.6 off, 10.5/11.3 On, 8.2/8.9 Lowest. Reflex on top adds nothing. With a cap the card holds, there is no queue and pacing waits nothing.
- Frame counter (Display tab, saved as fpsCounter=off|basic|detailed, fpsCounterCorner, fpsCounterDim): Basic = frame rate, small, in a corner; Detailed adds frame time, 1% and 0.1% lows of the last 10 s (average frame rate of the slowest 1% / 0.1% of frames) and a 2.5 s frame time graph (green, yellow for longer frames, red for stutters). Corner: top left/right, bottom left/right (top right moves under the status effect icons). Dim for OLED: see-through, no backing, moves a pixel every two minutes against burn-in. Same size at any GUI scale; hidden by F3 (which has its own) and F1. Each frame is timed start to start, so every wait counts (frame cap, Reflex, the card).

## start_here/playing_together | Playing Together
- World only runs near players. If one logs off or travels far, their base stops: farms, machines, trains in those chunks (see glossary/chunk) freeze until someone returns. Keep running with a Chunk Loader (see industry/power_loader).
- Trains stop at unloaded ground; Brass Chunk Loader on the train or at a station (see industry/power_loader).
- Mob farms still need a player nearby: mobs only spawn around players, chunk loaders or not.
- Night: one player in bed skips it; the other can keep working.
- Finding each other: share a waypoint to chat from the map (see home/maps), or check the other player's spot on it.
- Dying: Death and Graves (see survival/death_and_graves); a friend can fetch your grave.
- Backups: Simple Backups saves a full world copy every 2 hours, keeps last 10, in the simplebackups folder (next to the world on a server, in the game folder in singleplayer). Restoring: server guide. Server can also back up before a pack update (see server guide).
